# Annotations

> The spiritual guide Copeland found behind nature’s mask was a creature that, paradoxically, ignored us and spurned our illusions, Godless to the core.

> Because of their vast territories and the distances they travel, these solitary boreal mammals are uncommon everywhere, but they’re not finicky or fragile, because the wolverine, both a hunter and a scavenger, subsists on prey it runs down and on carcasses it sniffs out beneath the snow.

> A rule of thumb is that that any wilderness area that maintains its snow cover until May provides habitat for wolverines – barring, of course, human encroachment.

> It is the penchant of young males – wolverines as well as humans – to leave home and take off on long tangents to faraway territories and begin their lives anew.

> ‘Wanderlust is a huge component of wolverine ecology,’ he said. ‘Cheryl would say that I have it too.’ Until recently, the two never lived anywhere for more than seven years. ‘After seven years, we’d say we’ve had it, we’re anxious, it’s time to move.’

> Simultaneously, in the classroom, Charles Darwin’s theory of natural selection struck him like a coup de foudre. Copeland responded not only to its pitiless interpretation of survival – ‘nature, red in tooth and claw’, as Alfred, Lord Tennyson wrote – but also to its contrast with organised religion, which to him was just as severe. Natural science posed a radically different model of existence than the one he had been taught.
